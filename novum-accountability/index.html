NOVUM WASTED
MY TIME.

OVERPROMISED. UNDERDELIVERED.

I arrived for an advertised $1,800 nicotine study believing I had been accepted. Once I was there, I was told only two of 18 people would get primary spots. I became one of six alternates. After about three days, I was sent home with $225.

I was then told being an alternate guaranteed me a spot in the next study, which I understood offered $5,200. On November 17 I called about the December 20 study. I was told it was already full and I could not participate.

My opinion: Novum wasted my time. They overpromised and underdelivered.

This is a first-person account and criticism. My understanding of the promise and what staff told me have not been independently verified. The advertised full-study compensation is not presented as automatically owed to an alternate. Novum has an open invitation to respond.

What happened

Three days. A lottery. A broken expectation.

Here is my account of what I was told, what I experienced, and what I want explained.

Before November 6, 2026

I understood that I was accepted

I came for a nicotine study advertising $1,800 for completing the protocol. I say I was not told in advance that I would be entered into a lottery for one of only two primary places.

November 6–9, 2026

A lottery among 18 people

After arriving, I was told 18 people were being considered for just two primary positions. I became one of six alternates and remained at the facility for about three days.

November 9, 2026

Released with $225

I was sent home as an alternate and received $225. My complaint is about how the expectations, selection process, and payment terms were communicated before I gave up my time—not an unsupported assertion that I earned the entire completion payment.

Before leaving

Staff told me a future study place was guaranteed

I say I was promised a place in the next study because I had been selected as an alternate. I understood that next opportunity would pay $5,200 for completion.

November 17, 2026

“Full”—33 days before December 20

When I called to screen for the December 20 study, I was told it was full and I couldn't do it. The earlier study was scheduled to end November 12—just five days before my call. A study can fill early, but that does not explain the guarantee I say I was given.

My personal attendance and payment documents, consent terms, and any written promise of priority placement have not yet been published on this site.

My opinion

Stop wasting people's time.

Research volunteers arrange their lives around the opportunities they are offered. It matters whether an invitation is a confirmed place, an alternate position, or merely a chance at a lottery. And it matters when someone is promised priority for a future study.

Don't invite people to plan around a study without explaining the real selection odds. Don't promise alternates a guaranteed next opportunity unless you're prepared to honor it.

That is my criticism of Novum based on my own experience. I'm asking for clarity, accountability, and a documented response—not asserting that an investigation has established wrongdoing.
